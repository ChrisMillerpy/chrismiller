One question, three levels of answer

I tutor maths from GCSE to undergraduate. Here is the same problem the way I'd teach it to a Year 10, a sixth-former and a STEP candidate.

Problem · add up the whole numbers from 1 to $n$
GCSE

Write it twice

$$\begin{aligned}S &= 1 + 2 + \dots + n\\ S &= n + (n{-}1) + \dots + 1\\ 2S &= n(n+1)\end{aligned}$$

Every column adds to n+1, and there are n columns. No formula to memorise!

A-level

Prove it by induction

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n+1} k = \frac{n(n+1)}{2} + (n+1) = \frac{(n+1)(n+2)}{2}$$

Base case, assume, show. The exam wants the conclusion sentence written out.

STEP / university

Telescope it, then generalise

$$\sum_{k=1}^{n}\big[(k{+}1)^2 - k^2\big] = (n{+}1)^2 - 1 = 2S + n$$

Same trick with cubes gives Σk². Now try Σk³ yourself…
